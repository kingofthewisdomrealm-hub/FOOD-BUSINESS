# First market: San Antonio

**Date:** August 2026
**Decision:** start in San Antonio. Houston is the scale market. Austin is a later high-price site, not cart one.

The product and unit economics in this repo do not change by city. Permits, produce, commissary rent, and who already sells fruit cups do.

---

## 1. Scorecard

Scored 1–5. Higher is better for a first cart.

| Factor | San Antonio | Houston | Austin | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mexico produce pipeline | 5 | 4 | 3 | SA sits on I-35 / I-37 fruit coming north. SAWPM (Loop 410) is built as a Mexico–Texas hub. Houston Produce Center (Produce Row) is large but one hop farther from the border. Austin buys through distributors. |
| Commissary cost / availability | 5 | 4 | 2 | SA ~$250–$500/mo, published Metro Health list, little wait. Houston ~$300–$700, 60+ kitchens. Austin ~$350–$800 and waitlists. |
| Heat / thirst demand | 5 | 5 | 4 | All three work. SA and Houston stay hot longer. |
| People who already want chile-lime fruit | 5 | 4 | 3 | SA has *fruterías* (Los Trejo and others) and a Mexican street-food language. That is proof of taste, not proof the gym-and-campus slot is taken. |
| $10 price without a fight | 3 | 4 | 5 | Austin pays. Houston office parks pay. SA neighborhood corners will try to talk you to $7. Do not vend those corners. |
| Permit friction after 1 Jul 2026 | 4 | 3 | 3 | One DSHS Type II license statewide. Cities still control parking, fire, and downtown. Houston is dual city/Harris County in practice. Austin is competitive for pads. |
| Incumbent *fruteros* on sidewalks | 3 | 3 | 4 | Do not park on their corners in any city. SA and Houston have more of them. |
| First-cart capital efficiency | 5 | 3 | 2 | Cheapest kitchen, shortest produce run, fewer competing trucks. |
| **Total** | **35** | **30** | **26** | |

**San Antonio is cart one** because produce and kitchen cost are the two things that kill a fruit cart, and this city is cheapest on both. **Houston** is where a second cart goes when the first one is boringly profitable. **Austin** is where you charge $12 at UT / Domain / a gym cluster after the jig and the brand exist.

## 2. What already exists in Texas

These carts are not unknown here. They are just not a chain.

| Form | Where | Price signal | Lesson |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Neighborhood *frutería* storefront | San Antonio (e.g. Los Trejo: vaso de fruta ~$6.50+) | $6–$9 | The taste is already native. Competing on their parking lot is stupid. |
| Snack / raspa / fruit hybrid | San Marcos and similar (Yoyi’s: fruit cups with chamoy) | ~$8 | Menu creep (paletas, micheladas) is the trap. Stay fruit. |
| DFW *fruterías* | Richardson and elsewhere (Cano: fruta con chile ~$3.50–$8.50) | Cheap | Storefronts bundle ice cream and elote. A cart cannot. |
| Informal carts | Latino corridors in all three cities | $7–$10 | Same as LA: livelihood, knife skill, thin net. Do not price-war. |

The hole is the same as in the working model: **permitted, cold-held spears on private-property lunch pads** — gyms, hospitals, campuses, office lots — where $10 is a snack, not an insult.

## 3. San Antonio operating facts

### Produce

Buy the same day. Two wholesale nodes:

1. **San Antonio Produce Terminal** — 1500 S Zarzamora St (West Side, 1951 terminal). Traditional houses, early morning.
2. **San Antonio Wholesale Produce Market (SAWPM)** — 9342 SE Loop 410. Marketed as the city’s Mexico-linked *central de abasto*. Tenant mix includes fruit/veg houses plus dry goods.

Walk both at 6 a.m. in week one. Open an account with one house. Do not buy mango at H-E-B once you are past the pop-up.

Houston backup if you expand: **Houston Produce Center**, Produce Row (south of downtown, ~56 acres). Austin: restaurant depot or a Houston/SA house that already trucks north.

### Commissary

HB 2844 **does not** mean you skip a kitchen.

- DSHS (25 TAC 226): if the unit fails the **Central Preparation Facility / Commissary Exemption Checklist**, it must report to an approved CPF for water, wastewater, storage, and cleaning. **A house is not a CPF.**
- Pushcarts still need potable water, a wastewater tank **≥15% larger**, a hand sink, and a warewashing story (3-comp or spare utensils if the inspector allows it).
- San Antonio Metro Health still cares where you park overnight and dump tanks. They publish an approved commissary list. Budget **$250–$500/month**.

Plan as if you need a commissary. If DSHS later grants an exemption because the cart is fully self-contained, you still want a wash sink and a stainless table for the pineapple corer. That lives at a kitchen, not in a driveway.

### Where you may stand

City of San Antonio (still in force after the DSHS license):

- **No vending from residential property**
- **300 feet from a school property line** one hour before, during, and one hour after school
- Downtown Business District needs a **CCDO** special permit — skip downtown at launch
- City parks need Parks & Recreation permission — skip until you have a letter
- Unit must stay **readily movable** (no tying into site power/water)
- Employee toilet must be designated and accessible during hours — write that into the site letter (gym, hospital, brewery restroom)

**Launch sites (private property, written permission):**

1. UTSA / downtown college lunch (off the 300-ft school buffer)
2. Medical Center / hospital campus
3. Gym / CrossFit cluster on a commercial lot
4. Pearl / brewery night (after the weekday lunch is proven)
5. Saturday farmers market or youth sports complex

Avoid: West Side sidewalks next to existing carts, Fiesta-only fantasies, mall indoor courts.

### License class

DSHS Type **II** is the working assumption: pushcarts assembling TCS food for immediate consumption (snow cone / hot dog / deli class). Type III is cooking on the unit. Confirm on the phone before you pay $1,018. Cutting melon on the cart can still be argued; the hybrid model (core at CPF, finish on cart) is how you keep it Type II.

Fees (DSHS, 2026, including Texas Online): Type II application **$618** + pre-licensing inspection **$400**. Routine inspection **$400** shows up at renewal.

## 4. 90 days, San Antonio version

Same sequence as [working-model.md](working-model.md) §10, with city plugs:

| Window | SA-specific |
| --- | --- |
| Days 1–14 | LLC in Texas. Call DSHS (512-406-2644) and San Antonio Metro Health. Walk Zarzamora and SAWPM. Get two commissary quotes from the Metro Health list. Eat fruit cups at two *fruterías* and any cart you find. Time the cut. |
| Days 15–30 | Commissary hours + **one** farmers market or one gym-lot pop-up with a letter. Target 40 cups, twice. |
| Days 31–60 | Lock a weekday Medical Center or campus-adjacent lot. Start DSHS Type II plan review. Buy the Nemco pineapple corer if pineapple is the delay. |
| Days 61–90 | Permitted cart. 20 days. ≥50 cups/day, COGS ≤ $3.50, melon discard < 8%. |

If San Antonio cannot hit 40 cups at a decent pad, the site is wrong. Do not “fix” it by moving to Austin yet. Try a second SA lunch lot first.

## 5. Decision

Start in **San Antonio** because the fruit is closer, the kitchen is cheaper, and the taste is already in the city — just not on the pads that will pay $10 without a sidewalk war.

Do not wait for a mango machine. Do not buy a truck. Do not vend a school curb. Prove cups on a private lot, then put the fridge on wheels.
